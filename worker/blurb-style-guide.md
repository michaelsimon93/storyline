# Blurb style guide

**Status: first version, draft for Michael's product review.** The curation step puts this whole file in the blurb prompt every day, so editing it changes the next day's blurbs. The examples below are invented players, so no real person is misquoted.

## Voice
- Fan-first and punchy: say why a fan should care *tonight*. Write like a friend who watches every game, not a broadcaster and not a bettor.
- One idea per blurb. Lead with the player and the storyline, not the stat line.
- Plain words. No jargon, no hype stacking, no exclamation marks, no emoji, no all-caps.
- Never betting advice: don't tell anyone what to pick, and don't say how likely a pick is. The card already shows the line and the price.

## Length
- At most 200 characters, counting spaces (the limit in the rules is the one that counts). One line, plain text. Aim for 110 to 170.
- The card shows about three lines, so don't spend characters on the player's name twice.

## Banned words
Never use these (or their plurals and endings). They read as betting talk.

- lock
- hammer
- smash
- bet
- betting
- wager
- parlay
- guaranteed
- free money
- sure thing

## Facts
- Use only the facts you were given for that card, and cite the signal id behind every claim. If you can't cite it, don't write it.
- A fact marked `attributed` (a post from an approved account) is reporting, not fact: name the account by its handle, as it appears in the link, and say it as theirs ("Per @ShamsCharania, ..."). Never write it as plain fact.
- A fact whose `slateDate` is before the slate day is dated. State it as history ("over his last five games"), never as news from tonight.
- With no facts to cite (the card is `market_only`), write a short, plain blurb from the matchup, the line and the price alone. No streaks, no stats, no news.

## Story types
One good and one bad example for each. Good ones are fan-first, specific and grounded; bad ones are betting talk, vague or ungrounded.

### hot_streak
- Good: "Jordan Reyes has hit four or more threes in five straight, a career-best run. Can the heater survive a big, switch-everything Raptors defense?"
- Bad: "Reyes is a lock to smash his three-point line again tonight. Hammer the over." (betting talk, no reason, nothing a fan can check)

### back_from_injury
- Good: "Sam Okafor returns after six missed games, and the Heat have looked lost without him. How many minutes does he get, and how fast does he find his shot?"
- Bad: "Okafor is back, which is big news tonight!" (no detail, and if the return was reported days ago it isn't tonight's news)

### backup_steps_in
- Good: "With Dana Whitfield out, Lee Park slides into the starting five. He's played 30 minutes only twice this year. This is his shot to show he belongs."
- Bad: "Park will get all of Whitfield's minutes and score 20, easy money." (a prediction presented as fact, and betting talk)

### market_only
Used when there are no facts to cite.
- Good: "Reyes and the Bulls host the Raptors tonight. The line is four threes, and it's priced as a real toss-up."
- Bad: "Reyes has been on fire all month, so four threes is easy." (no fact was given to support it)
